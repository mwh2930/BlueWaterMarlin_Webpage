A field guide to the sources

One ocean.
Different kinds of information.

Some products begin with an instrument measurement. Others combine measurements into an analysis, estimate future conditions, or describe the seabed. Knowing the difference helps you interpret what a source can tell you.

Observation

A measurement at a place and time. Satellites can have cloud gaps; instruments have limits.

Analysis

Measurements combined or reconstructed on a grid. A filled cell may be an estimate.

Forecast

A model estimate for a future time. Its issue time and valid time describe different things.

Reference layer

Chart, coastline or terrain context. Detail and revision schedules vary by source.

01 / Ocean

Satellite observations & analyses

The product matters as much as the satellite. These feeds include both observed and reconstructed information.

NASA / JPL · NOAA CoastWatch

Sea-surface temperature

MUR v4.1 combines satellite and in-water measurements into a daily, global sea-surface temperature analysis. It is a Level 4 foundation-temperature product; each cell is an analysed estimate, even where the map looks continuous.

Product
MUR-JPL-L4-GLOB-v4.1 · jplMURSST41
Type / grid
Analysis · 0.01° (about 1 km)
Timing / extent
Daily · global ocean; recent fields can be revised.

Read its limits. Grid spacing is not a guarantee of feature accuracy or a fresh observation in every cell. The field timestamp is the nominal analysis time. Publication and delivery occur later.

These data were provided by JPL under support by NASA MEaSUREs program.

Copernicus Sentinel-3 · NOAA CoastWatch / PolarWatch

Chlorophyll & ocean color

Ocean-color sensors measure reflected light. Processing estimates chlorophyll concentration from that signal. The source collection includes Sentinel-3A OLCI observations and a separate VIIRS DINEOF reconstruction.

OLCI
noaacwS3AOLCIchlaDaily · Level 3 satellite product · daily, 0.0375° (about 4 km), global grid.
VIIRS DINEOF
noaacwNPPN20VIIRSDINEOFDaily · reconstructed analysis · daily, about 0.0833° (9 km), global grid.
Regional VIIRS
noaacwN21VIIRSchlaSectorUSDaily and noaacwN20VIIRSchlaSectorUSDaily · satellite products · daily, 0.0075°, US-sector grid (approximately 0–60°N, 142.38–52.39°W).
Coverage
Grid extent is not the extent of valid observations; clouds and quality flags leave gaps.

Read its limits. Clouds and retrieval quality can remove observed cells. DINEOF estimates missing values from spatial and temporal patterns; a reconstructed cell is not a new cloud-free measurement. NOAA identifies these products as experimental.

Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2026. Ocean-color products distributed by NOAA CoastWatch and PolarWatch; originating sensor and product credits remain with their providers.

NOAA / NESDIS · NOAA AOML

Sea height & surface currents

Satellite radar altimeters provide sea-height measurements. Blended sea-level anomaly products supply broad ocean structure; NOAA AOML provides a separate geostrophic-current analysis derived from altimetry and climatology.

Sea-level anomaly
noaacwBLENDEDsshDaily · daily blended analysis · 0.25° global grid, relative to the DTU15 mean sea surface.
Currents
miamicurrents · eastward and northward velocity · daily, 0.2° grid.
Current extent
Approximately 64.7°S to 64.7°N.

Read its limits. Geostrophic flow is an estimate of a component of ocean circulation. It does not describe every tidal, coastal or wind-driven motion. A broad sea-height anomaly is not a direct temperature measurement.

Data courtesy of NOAA; Sentinel data courtesy of Copernicus Program; Generated using AVISO+ products.

University of South Florida · NOAA AOML

Sargassum / floating-algae index

The Alternate Floating Algae Index (AFAI) is a reflectance-based signal sensitive to floating material such as Sargassum. This feed contains seven-day cumulative fields from USF, distributed by NOAA AOML.

Product
noaa_aoml_atlantic_oceanwatch_AFAI_7D
Type / grid
Satellite-derived composite · 0.015° grid.
Time / extent
Seven-day accumulation · 0–38°N, 98–38°W.

Read its limits. A cumulative field combines different observation times. Sun glint, clouds and thick aerosols can saturate the signal. AFAI alone does not establish species, biomass or a current patch boundary.

02 / Atmosphere

Weather & tide forecasts

Forecasts describe a valid time and location. Their publication time is a separate part of the picture.

NOAA National Weather Service · MET Norway

Marine wind & pressure

NOAA NWS grid forecasts provide wind in supported US areas. MET Norway’s Locationforecast 2.0 supplies a global forecast source, including wind and pressure. NWS forecast grids are approximately 2.5 km. MET Norway’s global model is approximately 9 km, with regional differences; forecast intervals vary by service and region.

NWS service
api.weather.gov · point lookup and forecast grid data.
MET service
Locationforecast 2.0 / compact · forecasts by coordinate.
Type / timing
Forecast · use the issue time and the interval for which the forecast is valid.

Read its limits. Modelled winds may differ from local conditions, especially near shore or rapidly changing weather. Service coverage and delivery are not guaranteed.

Based on data from MET Norway, licensed under CC BY 4.0. BlueWater Marlin selects forecast intervals, converts units and presents derived displays; these are not MET Norway’s original presentation. NWS-sourced government information is not claimed as BlueWater Marlin copyright.

NOAA CO-OPS

Tide predictions

The Center for Operational Oceanographic Products and Services publishes station-based predictions of high and low water. The requested product is predictions, with a high/low interval.

Service
CO-OPS Data API and station metadata API.
Type / coverage
Tide prediction · locations in the NOAA prediction-station network.

Read its limits. A nearby station does not establish the tide at an offshore location. Prediction heights depend on the stated vertical datum; wind and pressure can affect actual water levels.

03 / Seafloor & coast

Charts, depth & coastlines

Reference layers give geographic context. A detailed-looking map may include modelled terrain, generalized coastlines and survey data of different ages.

GEBCO · NOAA NCEI · Scripps / UC San Diego

Bathymetry & terrain

GEBCO 2026
Global terrain and shaded relief · 15 arc-second grid. Product & attribution · Terms of use.
ETOPO 2022
NOAA global relief model · ETOPO_2022_v1_15s, 15 arc-second grid. Product record · Dataset citation.
SRTM30+ v11
Global topography and bathymetry reference model · srtm30plus_LonPM180, 30 arc-second grid. Source & documentation · Dataset license.

Read their limits. These are reference models, with inputs from different surveys and estimation methods. Grid spacing does not establish the density of actual depth measurements. Vertical datums may differ. GEBCO must not be used for navigation or safety at sea.

SRTM30+ has separate commercial-use terms: its distributed license directs commercial users to UC San Diego Technology Transfer. Attribution on this page does not itself grant permission to use a dataset.

NOAA Office of Coast Survey

Nautical chart context

The NOAACharts map service supplies nautical-chart imagery. ENC Direct to GIS (general, coastal and approach layers) supplies vector depth contours and soundings where supported. Coverage, compilation scale and revisions depend on the source. A third-party display does not replace current official nautical charts.

Timing, coverage and uncertainty

Observation time, analysis time, forecast issue time, forecast valid time and download time are different. Source grids can include missing, reconstructed or revised values. Delivery, geographic coverage, continuity, accuracy and fitness for a particular purpose are not guaranteed. Provider specifications describe their products; they do not establish the accuracy of a BlueWater Marlin result. The sources listed here may vary with the layer, region and app version.

Educational use and fishing planning

BlueWater Marlin is a planning tool, not a navigation system. Do not use this page or its data for navigation, collision avoidance or safety-of-life decisions. Verify conditions against current official nautical charts, marine forecasts and appropriate onboard instruments. All diagrams on this page are conceptual illustrations, not live data.
