Round 4 — remediation agent

Eight findings, five of them make the page better.

Read as a punch list this is a bad morning. Read as an edit list it is mostly upside: the true annual saving is a stronger number than the false one, the honest support sentence is more convincing than the absolute one, and the attribution line the licences require doubles as the credibility strip the page was already reaching for. Two items are genuine work — a font swap and an image right. Nothing here needs a redesign.

Round 3 — copyright and claim agent, full scope

Verdict: STOP. Eight findings, four of them blocking.

Reviewed both pages as they stand — site copy, pricing disclosure, the eddy citations, third-party data attribution, fonts and imagery. The claim discipline is holding where it counts: fish-finding is refused explicitly, no accuracy figure is quoted, every layer wears its pass date, and the Readability tense is still driven by the data. What stops the page is a price sentence that does not survive arithmetic, one tense contradiction, and two rights problems. The first two are single-line edits. The other two — the font licence and the hero photograph — need a decision and a document, not a rewrite.

Stop level

FALSE

“Five months’ price for twelve.”

$99.99 ÷ $24.99 is exactly four. The annual tier is four months’ price for twelve, and the stronger number is the true one. A pricing overstatement is the one error a subscriber can check on the paywall itself, and it undermines every honest figure around it.

Instead: “Four months’ price for twelve.”

TENSE

“Every forecast engineered is graded against the next satellite pass, misses included.”

The closing line says grading happens. Straight answers, four sections above, says the record “is being built in the open and will be published.” One of the two is wrong, and a reader who scrolls the whole page sees both. If the scoring pipeline is not in a shipped build, the close is an unshipped feature stated as fact.

Instead: “Every forecast will be graded against the next satellite pass, misses included.”

LICENCE

SF Pro Display is first in the font stack.

Apple licenses the SF families for designing and developing on Apple platforms. A marketing website is not that, and the licence does not cover shipping it as web body type. Inter is already loaded and is OFL — the fix is to stop asking for SF first, which costs nothing visually for non-Apple visitors and changes almost nothing for Apple ones.

Instead: lead the stack with -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont (the sanctioned system-UI route) then Inter.

RIGHTS

assets/hero.jpg has no known provenance.

It arrived inside the original file as a data URI, so this review cannot establish who shot it or under what licence. A billfish photograph is exactly the kind of image that gets licensed per-use by marine photographers, and stock enforcement in this niche is active. Until the licence is on file, treat it as unclear rather than cleared.

Action: produce the licence or receipt, or replace it with owned imagery before launch.

Fix level

DISCLOSE

Terms and Privacy under the pricing table both point at #.

The subscription disclosure text is complete and correct — title, length, price, renewal, cancellation path — but two of the required links do not resolve, and neither does the Privacy Policy link on the support form or Privacy in the footer. An unresolvable disclosure link is the same as a missing one for App Store review and for consumer law.

PRIVACY

“We use your address to answer you and nothing else.”

The support form posts to a third-party form processor, so the address does reach a processor before it reaches you. The sentence is true in intent and incomplete in fact — and it sits on a site whose headline stat is “Zero · accounts, trackers, ads.” Naming the processor costs one clause and protects the promise that carries the whole brand.

Instead: “Your message reaches us through our form provider, and we use your address to answer you and nothing else.”

ATTRIB

The Sources strip names five providers and satisfies none of their attribution terms.

Copernicus data carries a required attribution form, and GEBCO requires its own wording naming the compilation. NOAA and NASA products are public domain but their courtesy lines are conventional and cheap. Logos are also the wrong asset to improvise: the ESA, NASA and NOAA marks have usage rules that a marketing page can breach without meaning to.

Action: one attribution line under the strip carrying the Copernicus and GEBCO required wording, and confirm every mark on that row against its own brand guidance.

COMPARE

“The same public instruments the expensive services read.”

No competitor is named, which is right, but the clause still makes a claim about what competitors charge — and it is the one line on the page that sounds like it is arguing with somebody. The structural point is stronger without the adjective: the instruments are public, so the differentiation has to be the processing, which is exactly what the next line says.

Instead: “The same public instruments every service in this category reads.”

Voice — quick, and one of them is deliberate

  • “Do you fish B.I.L.L.?” opens and closes the page. It is a rhetorical-question header, which the voice rules cut — but it also reads as a chosen brand line rather than drift, so this is flagged for your call, not rewritten.
  • “No signal. No problem.” is the one header on the page that performs instead of reporting. Every sibling is a finding. “Pre-rendered before you leave the dock” keeps the register.
  • “Your season never lapses mid-bite.” Reaches for vernacular the rest of the page avoids. “Your season never lapses mid-season” says the same thing plainly.
  • “That’s the advantage provided by B.I.L.L.” Passive and abstract on the last line the reader sees, directly after a sentence that earns something concrete. Flagged rather than rewritten, in case the wording is yours.
  • The 3.5 cm stat card reads as our accuracy at a glance; it is Sentinel-6’s sea-height spec. The citation below is correct — put the instrument name in the card label too.

Cleared

No fish-finding claim anywhere, and one section refuses it by name. No accuracy percentage, no comparative, no catch-rate claim. “Real-time” appears once and only to say it does not exist. Eddy copy claims detection, never prediction, and never touches upwelling. Both hemispheres are handled in the physics rather than asserted. Every limitation on the page is followed by a response. The Chelton and Sentinel-6 figures are cited with their source, sample and method, and the citation quotes nothing beyond a title. Charts are labelled as illustrations, the vertical exaggeration is printed on the eddy diagram, and the chartplotter disclaimer appears in both the body and the footer of both pages.

Marketing agent — claim check

What can't go out as written.

VERIFY

“All six instruments, every region”

A coverage claim is false the moment one region isn't rendering. Confirm every advertised region is live today, or change it to the regions that are.

Instead: “All six instruments, in every region we render”

VERIFY

“Every forecast he draws is graded against the next satellite pass, misses included.”

Present tense on the closing line. If the scoring pipeline is in the source tree but not in a shipped build, this is an unshipped feature described as fact — and it is the sentence the whole brand rests on.

If unshipped: “Every forecast he draws will be graded against the next satellite pass, misses included.”

VERIFY

“Zero — Accounts, trackers, ads” and “No account, no email, no trackers”

The privacy promise holds only while there is no data relay and no opt-in upload. If that changed, it changes here, in the App Store listing and in the manual on the same day — not one surface at a time.

FIX

“3 days — How far ahead Bill projects”

A capability with its limit three screens away. The stat strip gets skimmed on its own, and on its own it promises a three-day forecast with no uncertainty attached.

Instead: “3 days — How far ahead Bill projects, inside a widening cone”

PASS

Deliberate rule breaks, kept.

“Do you fish Bill?” is a rhetorical-question opener, which the voice rules normally cut. It carries the product name, it is the whole campaign, and it teaches the acronym one line later. Kept as a choice. No exclamation marks, no hype vocabulary, no competitor named anywhere on the page, and every limitation is followed by what we do about it — which is the half most brands leave out.

NOTE

Subscription disclosure is complete.

Title, both lengths, both prices, renewal terms, cancellation path, terms and privacy links, all on the surface that offers the subscription. The new hero sub-line repeats price before the reader scrolls, which is the fence-free version of the same disclosure.

Market agent — where the category fails

Six failure points. Four are answered.

Every product here sells a retrospective observation to someone making a forward, irreversible, roughly $2,000 decision at 9pm the night before. That is the whole opening.

ANSWERED The data is old by the time it matters. Forward projection is the hero promise and the second section on the page. This is the only structural gap in the category and the page leads with it.
ANSWERED Composites blur the edge and nobody says so. The drag comparison shows observed against fill. The sharp edge is the product, so showing which pixels are real is the argument, not a footnote.
ANSWERED Nobody publishes how often the water was readable. Added: a Readability Index section, labelled an illustration, in future tense. The most quoted positive review in this category praises being told where not to go.
ANSWERED Seasonal pricing fences convert $50 customers into $0 customers. Monthly at a price that makes sense to switch off, annual that beats five months. Nothing on the page punishes fishing four months a year.
OPEN Offline is the normal state, and the page only claims it in prose. Cell dies around thirty miles; satellite data offshore is metered by the gigabyte. Directive to design: one screen showing the download-before-you-leave step and what is cached, on device, with no signal.
OPEN This audience is on iPhone by a wide margin, and the page never shows a phone. Directive to design: an App Store download button and the chart shown on a phone held on a bridge, not as a browser-width canvas. The canvases currently read as a web dashboard.
The moat question, still unanswered by anyone. Every input here is free public data, so there is no moat by default. The first product to close the loop between what it predicted and what was caught owns the category. Nothing on this page implies that loop exists yet, which is correct — but it is the roadmap item worth more than any layer.

Design agent — what changed in this build

Apple-side notes.

  • Navigation earns a right-side action.Brand left, links and one filled Download right. A centered row of links with no action is a table of contents, not a nav.
  • Price appears before the first scroll.One quiet line under the hero buttons. Hiding price until section nine reads as a fence.
  • Section rhythm loosened.88–96px vertical padding, two background tones only, dark tiles reserved for the engineering and closing arguments.
  • Scroll-reveal removed.Fade-in-on-scroll delays content for no gain and fails badly on a slow marine connection. Content is present at first paint.
  • Hero image is a real file now.Pulled out of the 320 KB inline data URI so it loads in parallel with the text instead of blocking the stylesheet.
  • Two tweaks exposed.Default chart layer, and whether the Readability section shows — so the market agent's addition can be A/B'd without touching the file.
