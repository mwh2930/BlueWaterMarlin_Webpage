What cloud cover changes.

Infrared temperature observations can be blocked by cloud. Blended radar-altimetry sea-height data may still provide eddy context, while a projection continues only where current and wind inputs are available.

Eddy science

How sea-height data reveals eddies.

Radar altimetry measures sea-surface height. Research has shown how broad height anomalies can be used to identify and track mesoscale eddies.

Engineering

How the chart is prepared.

Ocean feeds vary in resolution, cloud coverage, and publication time.

  • Displayed on the source grid.MUR temperature uses its native 0.01° analysis cells. Tap-to-read selects the nearest displayed source or analysis grid cell, with no app-side interpolation.
  • Projected forward.When current data is available, the break can be projected to the arrival hour. A matching wind forecast can be included.
  • Hemisphere-aware leeway.The current default applies 3% of wind speed at 15°, right in the Northern Hemisphere and left in the Southern Hemisphere.
  • Eddy candidates updated nightly.Estimated half-amplitude radius comes from blended sea-height data. Rim, break, and current can provide corroborating context.
  • Reduced offline mode.Previously loaded SST, water color, and sargassum grids can be restored from the on-device cache. Live updates and new areas need a connection.

Straight Answers

  • Clouds BlockCloud can block infrared observations for days. Cloud-affected areas may show translucent analysis or no observation, with the distinction kept visible.
  • Not a chartplotter.This is a planning tool. It doesn't replace a GPS or official charts. Verify against them, every time.
  • Works from published feeds.The ocean layers are not real-time; source ages vary. Changing ocean layers show source or analysis time where applicable.
