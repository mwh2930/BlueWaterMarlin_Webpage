Eddy science

A warm eddy is a hill of water.

Eight centimetres of extra height spread across ninety kilometres of ocean. The tilt of that dome is what turns the water around it — and radar can measure it through cloud.

Engineering

Five steps between the
satellite and your screen.

Raw feeds arrive coarse, cloud-holed and a day old. This is the work.

  • Rendered native.Temperature at the full 1 km (0.6 mi) it was measured. The edge keeps its shape.
  • Advected forward.The break rides current plus wind leeway to your arrival hour, in a cone that widens honestly.
  • Hemisphere-correct leeway.Three percent of wind speed, deflected right up north and left below the equator.
  • Eddies detected nightly.Sized by half-amplitude radius from sea height. When rim, break and current agree, three instruments confirm one edge.
  • No signal. No problem.Pre-rendered and cached on the device. Sixty miles out, radios off, the chart is there.

Straight answers

B.I.L.L. can't see fish
from space.

  • Clouds beat him.A front can black out an area for days. You get translucent fill, or you get nothing — never a confident picture of a measurement never taken.
  • He works off daily passes.Real-time satellite ocean data doesn't exist. Every layer wears the date of its pass, so you always know how old it is.
  • He won't quote a number he hasn't earned.The measured accuracy record is being built in the open and will be published with its sample size and method. Until then, silence is the honest answer.
  • He's not a chartplotter.This is a planning tool. It doesn't replace a GPS or official charts. Verify against them, every time.
