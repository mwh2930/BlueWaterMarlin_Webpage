Turn 2 · wireframes, not builds

Selling the science: a photograph
can't beat a forecast.

Every chart in this category sells a picture of the past. The page's job is to make a reader understand, from geometry rather than adjectives, why that fails at the moment money is committed — and what physics we run instead. Eight diagrams, one claim each. Build order I'd argue for: 2f (the lag, which is the whole indictment), then 2a and 2b (the physics that answers it), then 2g (the grade that proves it).

Grey and dashed on purpose: geometry and labels only, no color or type decisions yet. Every number here is a placeholder until it comes from a counted month.

2f

The lag, on one clock

Claim: the category's product expires before it is used. Mechanism: one timeline with the purchase decision marked on it. No competitor is named and none needs to be — the reader supplies that. This is the argument the whole page rests on, and it currently exists only as a sentence.

2a

The cone, drawn to scale

Claim: the forecast states its own uncertainty. Mechanism: a scale bar. A cone without miles on it is decoration; nine miles of spread across a ten-mile move is a forecast a captain can plan against — and it says the honest thing without quoting an accuracy figure we haven't earned.

2b

Leeway, as a vector sum

Claim: this is fluid physics, not image smoothing. Mechanism: set-and-drift, which every offshore captain already computes by hand. Mirror it for the southern hemisphere beside it and "both hemispheres" in the pricing table stops being a bullet point.

2g

Graded against the next pass

Claim: the forecast will be scored in public, misses included. Mechanism: show the scoring method before there is a score. It is the only way to make that promise concrete without publishing a number we haven't measured — and it is the same discipline as the Readability Index.

2c

1 km against 4 km, same break

Claim: resolution is the product, not a spec line. Mechanism: one curve drawn twice. "1 km" means nothing as a number; a staircase where a break should be means everything. Makes the Instruments section argue instead of list.

2h

What clouds stop, and what they don't

Claim: a cloudy week is degraded, not blind. Mechanism: two wavelengths, one cloud. It states the limit and the response in a single picture, which is what the copy rules require of every limitation.

2d

An eddy in cross-section

Claim: a warm eddy is a measurable hill of water, and we can find it. Mechanism: the absurd ratio, admitted on the drawing. Printing the exaggeration factor is the same honesty move as labelling the fill, and it earns the sentence next to it.

2e

Three instruments, one edge

Claim: confirmation across independent instruments beats a prettier single image. Mechanism: three lines converging inside a marked box. Says "six instruments" is a method, not a feature count — without claiming anything about fish.
